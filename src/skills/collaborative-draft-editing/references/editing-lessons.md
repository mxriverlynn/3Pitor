# Content-editing lessons

Lessons generalized from the writer's pairing runs on blog post drafts. Each run's history is in its feedback
record under the pairing record directory. IDs are stable and never renumbered; a new lesson takes the next number
above the highest ID in this file and in any series notes. Lessons that apply to one post series only live in that
series folder's `README.md` under `## Editing notes`; the first series moved G31, G32, G44, G45, and G51 there, which
is why those numbers are missing below.

## What the runs show

- "What is X?" pointer and term fixes are the most common correction, and they spread into numbers and study
  claims ("made what faster?", "4.7 times more often?", "how is commit history a way of measuring bus factor?").
- Jarring seams are second. Nearly every run ended with a request for a transition the run had not built.
- About one structural move per run: a section cut, merged, split, or moved. They are rare and expensive.
- Length follows clarity. Several runs ended near or above their starting length at the writer's direction.
- The writer hand-edits heavily between stops and leaves most suggestions unapplied.

## Structure

- G1. Cut a section whole when it breaks the link between its neighbors and adds nothing.
- G2. Merge sections covering the same ground; split a principle from a tool catalog.
- G3. Tools come condensed: a one-line intro, a short list of one or two lines per tool. The principle lives in
  its own prose, not in the tool list.
- G4. Each post stands alone. When a post belongs to a series, set up the series' terms briefly on first use
  instead of assuming the reader read the earlier parts.
- G35. Pitfall items say why the pitfall is a trap and what each path concretely costs or gains.

## Content cuts

- G5. Cut analogies that explain what a developer audience already knows.
- G6. Cut references with no follow-up: extra citations, client details, named terms never used again.
- G7. Cut incidental detail: study-design specifics, numbers the argument doesn't use.
- G8. Cut fluff, stock phrasing ("worth taking seriously", "the way these things start now"), and asides that
  narrate the writer's caveats or the post's own moves.
- G9. A thread repeated in several sections lives in one place.

## Claims and sources

- G10. Cut or soften unlinked superlatives and findings ("the standard way of measuring").
- G11. First mention of a researcher or study says who or what it is and why the reader cares; a later mention
  repeats the link and a few words about the study.
- G12. State findings and names plainly; never claim to extend others' research.
- G13. Claims backed only by experience lead with "anecdotal evidence and personal experience show…". A weak
  link is a reason to cut the claim.
- G14. Tool behavior claims match the tool's current docs, checked at edit time.
- G46. A number says what was measured, in plain words ("reviewed what Copilot wrote nearly five times as
  often", not "verified 4.7 times more often"); a named method says how it works.

## Voice

- G15. One voice per paragraph: no mid-paragraph "I" statements, no abrupt "my" to "you" shift.
- G16. The writer's story lives in the intro; body sections run in "you" or expository voice.
- G17. No statements about the writer or what's theirs ("the discipline is mine").
- G18. Frame the writer's own moves as realizations, not tools reached for.
- G43. People build understanding, never the AI; make the person the subject.

## Clarity

- G19. Every pointer names its referent ("that check", "the first one", "the trial" all drew "what X?").
- G20. One term for one idea within a section; don't switch metaphors mid-argument.
- G21. Don't lift another post's dek phrasing into a sentence where it's unclear on its own.
- G22. A lead sentence must not read as the opposite of its paragraph's point.
- G36. No metaphors that need decoding ("go in", "nothing is left standing"); say the plain thing.
- G37. Keep tense consistent within a sentence.
- G41. Once the writer picks a term, carry it through the post.
- G47. Plain comparatives: "with fewer errors", not "more correctly".

## Flow

- G23. Each section opens with a lead-in tied to the one before it; re-bridge after every cut or move.
- G24. Adjacent sentences don't repeat a word or idea.
- G25. The close hands off to the next post in plain words that echo its dek, or closes the series.
- G26. Finish with a whole-post seam read.
- G33. A section's topic arrives as a consequence of the previous section, never out of nowhere.
- G34. A list's intro sets up the problem before naming the list ("what pitfalls? why would they be tempting?").
- G38. Don't repeat a small word within a sentence ("for… for… for").

## Process

- G27. On "reformat", reflow only; leave the writer's hand edits untouched.
- G28. The writer often takes a whole section as one piece, and may skip overview stops once the pattern is set.
- G29. Most corrections are "what is this?" fixes; check G19, G11, and G46 before showing an edit. A "what is
  X?" question is a fix request: answer in one line, then rewrite the sentence to say it.
- G30. Commit after each closed concern. Every pass leaves the draft wrapped and every number or study linked.
- G39. Reflow never merges list items.
- G40. Cut what doesn't earn its place, but don't cut clarity to hit a number.
- G42. Apply a suggestion only when the writer asks for it.
- G48. The writer may ask the run to answer its own overview questions from these lessons, then review the result.
- G49. "fix typos" means mechanical fixes only; style suggestions stay open.
- G50. Several concerns may close together in one commit when the writer moves on without closing them.
