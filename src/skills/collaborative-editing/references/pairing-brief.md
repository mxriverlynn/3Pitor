# Loop brief for a content edit

The collaborative-editing skill runs its paired review by these rules. The standard request below is quoted word
for word in every session log's `## Request` section; it is the request every recorded run was built from.

## The standard request

As a content editor, pair with me on `{draft_path}`. The goal is to improve the post draft, cut content length, and
ensure it properly flows. Items to consider cutting: details that aren't meaningful, references that do not have any
follow-up or other purpose within the post, and general "fluff" or emotional language. Split this into concerns by
section (intro and each major ## section), give me an overview of each section before diving into each paragraph. Ask
me questions about what I do and don't like about a paragraph, and what edits I want to make. Be sure the edits
include changes to make the post flow properly with no jarring or sudden transitions between paragraphs or sections.

## Concerns

One prose concern per section, in draft order: the intro (title, dek, and paragraphs before the first `##`), then each
`##` section, then a final whole-post seam read that looks only at the joins between sections. When the extra goals
name a whole-post theme or structural change, plan it as its own concern ahead of the section concerns, applied across
every section, with each later section overview checking the theme.

## Pieces

This is a content edit of an existing draft, not new writing. Each section concern starts with an overview stop (what
the section does, questions keyed to its paragraphs, no edits), then one paragraph per stop, or the whole section as
one piece when the writer asks. Mark the structural moves the initial lessons pass deferred (a section cut, merge,
split, or move) as expensive to walk back.

## Stops

- **The plan stop** is the first turn after the initial lessons pass. It summarizes the pass, lists the concerns in
  order with the deferred structural proposals, highlights the passages the proposals touch, and asks the writer to
  accept or change the plan. Record the plan in the log as `## Plan (proposed)`, and mark it accepted when the writer
  accepts it.
- **An overview stop** says what the section does and asks the section's questions, one per paragraph that needs one,
  each highlighted. It makes no edits.
- **A paragraph stop** gives your reading of the paragraph, cites the lesson ID behind each point (for example G19),
  proposes its edits as before-and-after text, and asks what the writer likes, dislikes, and wants changed. Highlight
  each passage a question is about.
- **The next turn** starts from the writer's answer. Apply the edits the writer accepted, with the writer's changes,
  then log the answer, then move to the next stop. When the answer is a question, answer it and ask again; do not move
  on until the writer does.
- Check every edit against the lessons and the series notes before you show it. Follow the writer's reformat and
  "fix typos" requests the way the lessons describe, except that reformatting line breaks is the editor's job when the
  writer saves, so say so and leave line breaks alone.

## The session log

The log already holds the request and the initial lessons pass. Append to it rather than starting a new file:

- `## Plan (proposed)`: the concerns in order, with the structural proposals, then the writer's decision on the plan.
- `## Feedback log`: one entry per stop, in order, headed by the concern and piece, holding your reading with its
  lesson IDs, the writer's response in their words, and what changed.
- `## Close`: written when every concern is closed or the writer ends the run. It holds the estimated starting,
  post-lessons-pass, and final word counts, and what the plan named but did not reach.

Do not edit any lessons file or series notes during the review; the skill proposes new lessons after the close.
